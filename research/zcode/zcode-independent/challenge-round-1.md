## Mission-level challenges

### Z1 — The scoring rubric does not mirror the actual judging weights
Claude's approach-seeds rubric weights originality 20%, pain 20%, evidence 10%, Cloudflare fit 15%, feasibility 15%, demo 15%, adoption 5%. The official rubric (E-C014) is: originality+prototype quality 50% (tie-break on originality), multi-agent concurrency effectiveness 25%, UX 25%. Pain and adoption — 25% of our rubric — are scored nowhere by judges; originality is underweighted by 2.5x. Rankings produced under the current rubric will systematically favor well-evidenced-but-derivative approaches and disfavor weird-but-original ones, which is exactly the failure mode the 50% weight punishes. Improvement (tradeoff): re-rank with the official 50/25/25 mapped onto our axes (novelty+demo-quality ≈ 50%, coordination/concurrency coverage ≈ 25%, UX/demo ergonomics ≈ 25%), keep pain/evidence as a hard gate (kill factor) rather than a weighted score. Tradeoff: we lose fine-grained separation among well-evidenced approaches and must accept judge-mimicking coarseness; that is the correct loss because the rubric exists to predict judges, not to measure research quality. Falsification test: take the current top-3 seeds and the 3 most original seeds; if the official-weight ranking inverts the current one by more than 3 places, the rubric change was material and must be adopted before A01-A20 scoring.

### Z2 — The strongest evidence and the judged direction point at different products
Our best-evidenced pain is review burden / AI slop on OSS maintainers (E-C201..E-C233: curl ~20% slop and 5% valid rate, GitHub's own "Eternal September" admission E-C205, Ghostty auto-close E-C208, LLVM "extractive" E-C213, Faros +91% review time E-C228). But (a) a large policy bloc simply refuses AI content (QEMU E-C212, NetBSD E-C211, Zig E-C218, Gentoo E-C210) — no product can serve them; (b) GitHub is commoditizing volume controls fast (collaborator-only PRs E-C206, PR caps/kill-switch roadmap E-C226); (c) the judged concurrency axis (25%) has visibly weaker positive evidence — plain worktrees succeed for independent tasks (E-X007, E-X013), advisory coordination tools "haven't earned their keep" (E-C113), and informed agents ignore shared memory (E-C114). Both principals must state, per shortlisted approach, which side of this tension it sits on and why it survives the policy bloc (Z-kill below). This is not a reason to drop review-burden work: it is a reason to aim it at the AI-accepting minority with a verification product, not a moderation product.

The rules require the project to "enable multiple agents working on changes concurrently" (E-C011); they do not require a full forge. Six distinct products must not become six modules of one mega-platform (Codex X8 already resists this for one candidate; generalize it). ## Architecture-level challenges

### Z8 — Enforcement is post-hoc; design the whole platform as detect→act, not prevent
With no v2 receive-pack, no pre-receive hooks, and events arriving after push (E-C307/E-C308), any "policy-enforced proxy" (seed 11) is really: agents push to per-agent quarantine repos (E-C309 best practice), a Worker consumer inspects `repo.pushed` events, a DO arbitrates claims, and landing into canonical happens only via the platform's own controlled write path. Embrace it: the demo story "violator caught and quarantined in seconds; canonical history never touched" is stronger than an unenforceable prevention claim. Falsification: if a raw git client can corrupt canonical state through any documented path, the design fails.

### Z9 — Merges happen in our Workers or not at all
No merge/commit-create/ref-write in the binding (E-C305). Merge trains, stacked micro-changes, and tournament landing must either perform merges client-side (wasm git libs in Workers) and push results with scoped tokens (E-C305 createToken scope/TTL), or instruct the losing agent to reapply (bounded intent reapplication). Budget this in feasibility scores; it is the single most likely place for an Oct 13 surprise.

### Z10 — Demo constraints are design constraints
5-10 min video, no disparagement of discernible products including competitors (E-C012); finalists present live Oct 21 and the winner must be physically present (E-C015 — flag to orchestrator/user again under eligibility). Demo must visibly hit all five judged concurrency words (coordination, context preservation, review, conflict handling, plus concurrency itself) with ≥3 agents doing real overlapping work on small repos (1 GB / 128 MB limits, E-C304/E-C306). UX is 25% of judging and currently appears in no lane's plan; someone must own a UI workstream by shortlist time.
