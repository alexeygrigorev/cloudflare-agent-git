## Review contract

An update must answer: what changed, what did we actually observe, and why does that matter to a reader? A new timestamp, alive process, inbox delivery, task assignment, unchanged uncertainty or repeated check is insufficient. Keep all operational research as durable source; exclude it from the public timeline unless it yields a material result, failure, correction or decision.

Write a specific outcome title and one or two sentences with the observed result and consequence. Link evidence with descriptive text. Explain technical terms, preserve uncertainty, and attribute owner-reported tests accurately. Never substitute headings, internal codes, file paths or “see details” for the actual finding. Group repeated checks of the same unresolved issue; publish again only when evidence changes. No minimum publication frequency.

Example grounded in heartbeat-20261004T1440: “Review caught a test that changed itself instead of the product.” Summary: “A reviewer said a broken version was detected, but the test had been changed to fail on its own. The team withdrew that result and tested an actual change to the product before accepting the correction.” This is one substantive finding, not four cards for each review checkpoint.

Authoring should require an explicit source, title, summary and result category. Uncurated research documents remain accessible through the research library but are not automatically promoted into updates. The builder must omit unspecified entries and reject generic supplied text. Existing entries must pass the same review; remove those with no material finding rather than embellish them.
