## Write for readers, not for the team

Daily reports and site articles are for people who have never opened this repository. Internal documents can use the team's shorthand; published pages can't. Check each rule before publishing:

1. Open with what the experiment is and why it matters, in two or three sentences a newcomer understands: Cloudflare's Git platform competition, a team of AI coding agents researching what Git-like tools coding agents need, and the concrete problem (agents' changes clash, project copies fill the disk). Give that context before any detail.
2. No internal codes or identifiers in visible text: no approach IDs (A01, A16), protocol or evidence IDs (R12, E-A040, D-G25), revision hashes, file paths, session IDs or message IDs. Name the thing instead ("the collision radar idea", "the written test plan"). Links may point at pinned commits; their text may not show the hash.
3. No team role names or process words: not "principals", "heads", "lanes", "gates", "falsification", "cutoff", "fixture", "arm", "harness", "receipt". Write "the two lead agents", "the target set in advance", "the test that would rule the idea out", "evidence up to 02:24 UTC".
4. Don't use engine or tool names as codes ("a ZCode fixture", "Muse showed"). Introduce the agents once by model name, then say what the agent did ("the agent that reviews others' work", "one agent's test").
5. Explain each technical term the reader needs in one short clause at first use: worktree, dependencies, virtual environment, hardlink, Cloudflare Workers, Artifacts.
6. Link text says what the reader gets: "my instructions to the agents", not "USER-INSTRUCTIONS.md"; "a fact-check by Claude", not "journal-factcheck-2026-10-03.md".
7. Keep uncertainty and corrections, in ordinary words: "not validated", "nobody knows yet", "the first version said X; the evidence shows Y". Don't drop a limitation to make the sentence simpler.
8. No empty qualifiers that describe the writing itself: "in plain words", "in plain language", "to put it simply", "in human language", "honestly", "let me explain". Write plainly instead of announcing it.
9. Cold reader test before publishing: read every paragraph as someone who has never seen the repo or the team. If a paragraph needs a term, name or number that the article hasn't explained, rewrite it.

- Before: "A01, Live Integration Radar, was the primary candidate." After: "The front-runner was the idea of watching the agents' unfinished work and warning them about conflicts while they still work, not after they finish."
- Before: "Both arms produced compatible first commits and needed zero repairs." After: "They ran two setups under the same rules. In both, the agents' first changes fit together and nothing needed fixing."
- Before: "No candidate has passed the real gate yet." After: "No idea has passed the real test yet. That test needs two actual coding agents working at the same time on Cloudflare Workers, Cloudflare's service for running code."
- Before: "Both principals conditionally approved the R12 protocol v2.2, but only for three unscored engineering pairs." - Before: "The 20 ideas, in plain words" (title). After: "The 20 ideas".
